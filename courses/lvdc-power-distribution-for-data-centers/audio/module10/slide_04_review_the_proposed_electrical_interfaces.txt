Review the electrical interfaces before tracing the disturbance. Start with the source-to-load voltage contract. The fictional proposal needs continuous ranges, transient magnitude and duration, power capability, and the effects of the distribution path. Matching nominal labels do not close that question.

Next, confirm the polarity and earthing arrangement. Identify the intended normal-current conductors, the protective path, and the voltage stresses relative to earth. Determine how insulation monitoring and fault response work in that configuration. A generic product rating should not be assumed to cover every combination in the paper's review set.

Then inspect the rack boundary. Is the input feeding a conversion shelf, a junction arrangement, or a smart distribution unit? Where does downstream conversion occur? Who provides precharge, interruption, isolation, and reporting? Local buffering adds another energy source and another set of operating limits.

Write each unanswered item as a review action. For example, request an agreed source-load envelope with the required test evidence, rather than write only voltage compatibility missing. A precise action identifies the owner, the acceptance question, and the information needed to resolve it.

Keep the proposal's status visible. This scenario can teach the shape of a complete interface contract without inventing a settled numerical envelope. The reviewer should be able to tell what the architecture intends and what evidence is still required before that intent becomes a demonstrated operating capability.
