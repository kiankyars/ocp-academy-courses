A data center has obligations at its utility connection as well as requirements inside the facility. The point of common coupling is the electrical boundary used for the applicable grid-interface assessment. Voltage ride-through, power recovery, ramping, and power-quality requirements must be understood at that boundary.

Storage might keep an internal D C bus energized while the facility's behavior at the utility connection fails a required response. Conversely, a facility might meet a grid-interface obligation while one internal load loses continuity because its local distribution or cooling path is inadequate. The two outcomes must be reviewed separately.

Requirements depend on the connection agreement, network, jurisdiction, and applicable documents. A timing value or recovery rule from one setting should not be taught as a universal obligation for all installations. Identify the governing requirement before comparing a proposed converter or control strategy with it.

Map the boundary, the measured variables, and the equipment that controls those variables. Then ask how internal controls coordinate with the grid-side response. Storage, converters, and load admission can affect both sides of that discussion, but they do so through specific operating behavior.
