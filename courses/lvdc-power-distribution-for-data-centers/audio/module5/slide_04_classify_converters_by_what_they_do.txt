Converter names can hide several independent characteristics. A controlled converter can regulate relevant electrical behavior within its designed range. An uncontrolled conversion stage has different capabilities. Neither description, by itself, establishes whether the circuit is galvanically isolated.

Isolation is a separate property of the topology. A low-frequency transformer may sit before rectification, or a high-frequency isolated stage may sit within a multi-stage converter. The resulting power chain must still deliver the required output, handle abnormal conditions, and meet the intended insulation and referencing requirements.

Power-flow direction is another question. A bidirectional capability can be useful for storage integration or energy exchange, but it must be part of the equipment's actual design and control strategy. Do not infer that capability from a generic converter symbol.

The fault response deserves its own evidence. A device may regulate current during one condition while allowing capacitor discharge or a different current path during another. The system review needs the transient behavior, not just a normal-operation efficiency curve.
