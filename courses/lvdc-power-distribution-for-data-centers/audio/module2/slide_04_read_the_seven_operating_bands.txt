The seven voltage bands organize behavior, not just numbers. Read the ladder from low voltage upward. Band one represents blackout. Band two is a critical operating region. Band three is the nominal operating region, while band four allows temporary operation under defined conditions.

The next transition is important. Band five is an overvoltage protection region in which normal operation is not required. Band six concerns clamping, and band seven is prohibited. A device's ability to withstand or clamp a voltage must not be interpreted as permission to operate continuously at that voltage.

The boundaries need a time dimension and a statement of responsibility. A source may be expected to hold its output within one range. A load may need to remain operating through a defined excursion outside that range. Protection may act beyond another threshold. Those requirements can complement one another, but only when the values and durations are coordinated.

The diagram deliberately shows the functions without inventing a single set of numerical limits. Later proposals in the paper use different envelopes and contain unresolved items. Carrying numbers from one proposal into another would create a requirement the paper has not established.

Use the band model to structure a conversation. Which region should the system occupy normally? What happens during a disturbance? What must continue working, what may stop, and what must prevent damage? Ask those questions before accepting a color-coded voltage chart.
