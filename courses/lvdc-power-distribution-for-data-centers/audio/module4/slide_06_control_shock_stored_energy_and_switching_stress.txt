An electronic switch can report off while hazardous voltage remains somewhere in the circuit. Input or output capacitors may still be charged. Another source may backfeed the isolated section. A measurement or communication signal may also be unavailable or misleading during a fault.

Separate three ideas: the command sent to equipment, the state reported by its controller, and the independently verified electrical condition. Those states should agree in a well-designed system, but a command alone is not evidence that the physical condition is safe for maintenance.

Interruption also creates switching stress. Current flowing through inductance cannot disappear without an associated voltage response. Clamping and energy-absorption elements must be coordinated with the interruption function and the equipment's withstand capability. A fast switch therefore remains part of an energy-management problem.

The conceptual diagram traces possible remaining energy from capacitors and alternate sources. It is intended to help reviewers identify the required isolation, discharge, and verification functions. It is not a sequence for performing live work or a substitute for an approved maintenance procedure.

For a real installation, define the safe state, the means of reaching it, and how it is confirmed. Include possible voltage rebound, backfeed, and the response when a required measurement is unavailable. The protection design and the operating procedure need to agree on the same physical boundaries.
