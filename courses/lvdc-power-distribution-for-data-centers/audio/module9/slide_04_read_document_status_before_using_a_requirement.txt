Document status changes how a statement should be used. A published standard has an issued edition and a defined scope. A draft or project proposal is still developing. An outline of investigation can provide an evaluation framework for a specific purpose. A certification requirement decision operates within a particular certification context.

These categories are not interchangeable labels for the same authority. A proposed requirement can be important for planning while remaining different from the basis of a current certificate or an adopted installation rule. The correct language preserves that distinction.

The paper reflects a changing standards landscape. Before relying on a status statement, check the issuing organization's current publication or certification information. Record the date of that check, because a draft discussed in one source may have become a published document later.

The practical habit is to attach three facts to a cited requirement: the issuing body and document, its current status and edition, and the scope that makes it relevant. That is more durable than memorizing a list of numbers without knowing what they authorize.
