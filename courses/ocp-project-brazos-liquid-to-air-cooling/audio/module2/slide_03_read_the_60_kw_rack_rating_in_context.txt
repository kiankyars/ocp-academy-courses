The overview table gives a useful starting point: sixty kilowatts of rack thermal load, nineteen gallons per minute of deionized-water flow, and twenty-five pounds per square inch of liquid pressure differential. It also lists an eight-degree approach temperature. These values describe the rack-level design target, not interchangeable ratings for every coolant or installation.

The airflow entry is approximately seventy-seven hundred cubic feet per minute at maximum fan speed, with zero hot-aisle back pressure. That condition matters. Additional resistance in the exhaust path can change the available flow. A site review therefore needs evidence for the installed air path rather than the headline airflow alone.

The detailed thermal table contains an unresolved condition issue. Its caption says nominal flow is twelve point eight gallons per minute. Yet the sixty-kilowatt column gives flow per kilowatt values that imply approximately nineteen point three to nineteen point five gallons per minute. Do not treat every load column as one constant-flow experiment.

The practical response is to keep the published values and their stated conditions visible, then request clarification before using that comparison to size an installation. A specification is useful evidence, and reading it carefully includes recognizing where two entries do not describe the same test condition.
