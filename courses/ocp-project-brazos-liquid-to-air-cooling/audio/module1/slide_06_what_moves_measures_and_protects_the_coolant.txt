A Brazos chassis brings several functions together. The pumps move coolant around the external loop. Check valves limit reverse flow through a pump that is not operating. The radiator transfers heat to air, while the fan array establishes the air path through it.

The strainers capture particles before they can continue through sensitive parts of the circuit. The specification lists three-hundred-and-twenty-five mesh, or forty-four microns. This is a component requirement within the design, not a complete water-quality or cleaning strategy. The expansion tank accommodates the loop’s expansion behavior within its specified pressure and temperature limits.

Sensors make the system observable. Supply and return temperatures describe the liquid’s thermal change. Pressure measurements support differential-pressure control. Flow measurements help establish whether circulation matches the requirement. Inlet and outlet air temperatures connect the air-side condition to the heat-transfer task.

Leak detection adds another kind of evidence: whether liquid has escaped its intended path. It cannot replace pressure, flow, or temperature monitoring. A loop may have insufficient cooling without an external leak, and a leak signal does not by itself explain the remaining thermal capacity.

Review these functions as a connected system. Moving fluid, transferring heat, filtering, accommodating expansion, and detecting abnormal conditions each answer a different operating question.
